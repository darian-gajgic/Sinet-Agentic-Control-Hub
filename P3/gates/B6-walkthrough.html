The give-work journey — walk this first (checkpoint 3)

A project born, activated from your Inbox, given work, and the work watched to Done — end-to-end on this seeded world. Sign in as op first (PIN above): creating a project is a person's act, and the door says exactly that if you skip this.

  1. Projects → "New project"

    Give it an id and a plain name; leave "Remote to clone" empty — that starts a fresh store. Register, and onboarding runs machine-side (register → store → scan → draft). The dialog names the onboarding task and its approval, and offers "To the Inbox — the approval finishes it".

  2. Inbox → the onboarding card

    The card says what it is about (the drafted danger zones), what approving records, and why approve is its only verb — there is no decline, because nothing yet undoes a registration; cancelling the onboarding task is the honest way out, and the card says so itself. Press approve.

    • Defect: an approve that errors, or a card that looks answerable with no working button.

  3. See the consequences

    On Projects the card flips to ACTIVE (capture v1 · conventions · danger zones). On the board, the Done column holds "Onboard …" under the project's own group.

  4. On the project card: "Describe a goal"

    The door opens pinned to the project and says the task will build on that project's world. Say what you want the way you'd tell a person, and send it.

  5. The interview — a form, not a chat

    Numbered question cards: answer, pick an option, or skip. "Proceed — turn open questions into assumptions" is legitimate — every skip becomes a LISTED assumption on the plan card, where you can still contest it. The page listens for what comes next; the same card also sits in your Inbox, so leaving the page loses nothing.

  6. The plan card — approve it

    What I understood · what you'll get · numbered steps each with "done when" · what I will NOT do · assumptions first · the price. Approve — the PIN step-up takes the PIN above and is never stored. Re-plan, Re-interview and Cancel are the other doors.

  7. Watch it on the board, then open its card

    The task carries its project from birth and moves Backlog → Executing → Verifying → Done as you watch. Its PLAN expander shows the plan's steps with live recorded progress. Click the card — the structured overlay window holds the whole story: the confirmed spec with acceptance criteria, the stage ledger with every decision you made, the receipts.

If a planner session dies mid-journey (it happened once on the verification walk that wrote this page): nothing is asked of you. The card stays in its column, the recovery ladder forks and re-drives the session at its next sweep, and the plan arrived 5 minutes 22 seconds after the crash — machine-only, no button pressed. Seeing that once is the platform keeping its word, not a defect to report.

The walk — eleven surfaces, in order

  1. Mission control

    0 running and 0 queued — by design. Everything seeded either waits on a person (9 parked runs with open asks — the "waiting on you" count and the inbox link) or is already done (1). Nothing seeded is dispatchable, so a zero here is the honest reading, not a broken feed.

    • Defect: a meter shows a confident number it cannot know. Absence must read as absence.
    • Defect: anything claiming to be running or executing right now.

  2. The live board

    Ten seeded tasks across five real columns — Backlog · Executing · Verifying · Needs attention · Done — grouped by project, horizontally scrolling, never wrapping. Every task sits in a declared column; nine carry a waiting on a person chip because their runs are parked on open asks, one is in Done. There is no "moonshot" column any more — that was a test artifact, and it left the demo world.

    • Executing/Verifying columns hold parked tasks whose stage is executing/verifying — the chip on the card, not the column, says nothing is actually running.

  3. Task detail — t-ship

    Open this task specifically. Of the ten seeded tasks, only t-ship ("Ship the release notes") and t-brief carry deliverables. Scroll to its Deliverables section — six rows, and that section is the door to step 9.

    • On any other task, "This task has produced no deliverables yet" is the correct answer.

  4. Fleet

    Per-person and per-model meters, budget remainders.

    • These render the real stores' answers here, not the fixture doubles — so figures may be absent where the committed fixture bodies show one. That difference is honest.

  5. The approval inbox

    Ranked cards with tier mechanics. The seed includes a conformance card (high tier, answerable) and an effect card that is deliberately not answerable by you — it is its owner's decision, and the card says so.

    • Defect: a card that looks answerable but has no working answer, or a not-answerable card that fails to say why.

  6. Settings

    Every setting, generated from the registry: clamp bounds, restart badges, audit history.

  7. Settings → "This device"

    The push-enrolment panel, on the same settings page.

  8. Chat

    Empty under the dev fallback. Sign in as alice (PIN above) and the seeded sessions appear. Checking both states is the point of this step.

  9. The review surface — seven deliverables

    This is the one the gate leans on. Each deliverable proves a different part of it. Start with d-site — it is the side-by-side diff.

    OpenTypeWhat it must show
    d-site code The real line diff across two revisions of site/release.tsx plus a deleted site/legacy.tsx. The toggle is labelled "Side by side" (the default) and "Inline" — not split/unified. Seven comments carry their placement status across the revision hop: exact line anchors, file-level fallbacks where the line moved, and one that lost its file entirely. One is already consumed by round 2.
    d-hero imageThe image pair — 2-up first, swipe one click away.

  10. Workforce

    The seed composes three workers, so this has rows here. A worker in draft with no active version must say why nothing is equipped, not render an empty card.

    • On a genuinely fresh host this page is empty, and that is also correct — "no standing army": machinery is composed when earned.

  11. Phone width — re-walk 1, 5, 6

    Narrow the window to a phone width. Phone-complete is a requirement for mission control, the inbox and settings. Diff review and settings-bulk only need to be desktop-comfortable.

Known before you start — do not debug these.

  • "Try it out" errors on the code deliverable. It answers {"error":"internal","detail":"project: git worktree: chdir projects/release-notes.git: no such file or directory"}. The seed mints revisions but never creates the project repository a preview launches from. S13.8 requires every type to answer a defined state (no-preview, requires-container-tier), so a raw internal error leaking a filesystem path is a finding for question C below — already logged, not yours to chase.
  • Meters and the price table differ from the committed fixtures. They render the real stores' answers here; the fixture world's doubles cannot reach a throwaway binary. Honest difference.
  • The tour and first-use hints are app behaviour, not seed data. "Show me around" in the header starts the tour; hints re-appear on a full reload.

What the gate needs from you afterwards

In your own words — free text is fine and is authoritative:

  1. Did anything look wrong, ugly, or confusing? Taste counts. The report specifically asks you to rule on relative-vs-UTC timestamps, and on whether the bundle feels heavy on a phone.
  2. Did any surface fabricate a number instead of saying it did not know?
  3. Did any control look pressable but do nothing?
  4. Anything missing you expected to be there?
