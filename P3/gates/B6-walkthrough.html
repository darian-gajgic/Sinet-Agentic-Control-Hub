The give-work journey — walk this first (checkpoint 3)

A project born, activated from your Inbox, given work, and the work watched all the way to Done — proven end-to-end on this world on 2026-08-17 (both endings below are photographed in P3/design/rework-screens/step4/). Sign in as op first (PIN above): creating a project is a person's act, and the door says exactly that if you skip this.

  1. Projects → "New project"

    Give it an id and a plain name; leave "Remote to clone" empty — that starts a fresh store. "What kind of tasks this project holds" is offered right here — declare "Build or change software" and no task in this project is ever asked the kind-of-work question. Register, and onboarding runs machine-side (register → store → scan → draft). The dialog names the onboarding task and its approval, and offers "To the Inbox — the approval finishes it".

  2. Inbox → the onboarding card

    The card says what it is about in plain words — the task family it will set, the drafted danger zones — what approving records, and why approve is its only verb: nothing yet undoes a registration; cancelling the onboarding task is the honest way out, and the card says so itself. Press Approve — activate this project.

    • Defect: an approve that errors, or a card that looks answerable with no working button.

  3. See the consequences — and know what a fresh store means

    On Projects the card flips to ACTIVE and shows exactly what the scan captured. On the board, Done holds "Onboard …" under the project's own group. Read the capture line on the project card. A store born empty has NO build/test commands — its card says "0 conventions · 2 danger zones" and no tests line — and that decides which ending your first software task gets (step 8 vs step 9). On this walk's world, parts-catalog already carries capture v2 · tests: make test (a Makefile seeded into its store, then re-scanned), which is why its task rode straight to Done.

  4. On the project card: "Describe a goal"

    The door opens pinned to the project and says the task will build on that project's world. Say what you want the way you'd tell a person, and send it. Left empty, the name field fills itself from your goal — the name the board will use is shown in the field before you send, so a task never wears a raw t-… id as its title. The task is born with its family already on the breadcrumb (op · software) — no kind-of-work card appears at all: the project declared it, and the running classifier covers the undeclared case. Seeing the six-choice family card on a clear goal into a declared project is a DEFECT now, not a step. While it reads your goal the page says so — its own clock ticking, never a bare spinner. The first card takes one to two minutes on the local models.

  5. The interview — a form, not a chat

    Numbered question cards, options as buttons, free text everywhere. Today's truth on wording (PH-1): questions arrive in the platform's standard wording and the page says so — a quiet "standard wording" line under the question, or one line for a whole round. The live-rephrasing seat has never fired in production (its silent failure is diagnosed; the backend fix lands right after this round) — so a round that reads fluent is the paid planner speaking, not rephrasing. Points you skip recap as one line naming what runs on defaults — counted honestly as checklist points (one skipped question can fan out into several), never a wall of rows. The Clearance meter appears only while it is asking — it measures how settled the must-knows are, so outside the questioning it measures nothing and is gone (a full meter beside open markers used to read as a contradiction). From the second round on, a "What it understood so far" recap sits above the questions: every settled point with where it came from — you answered, from the project record, or assumed — out loud — and the stakes header re-sizes as it learns (HIGH flipped to LOW on this walk once the goal proved trivial); the stakes chip now carries what its level means on sight. Proceed keeps what you typed and says so on the button — "Proceed — keep my N answers, assume the rest" — and every skip becomes a LISTED assumption on the plan card, where you can still contest it. Each answered round takes about a minute; between rounds the page shows what the machine is doing and how long it has been at it; the same card also sits in your Inbox, so leaving the page loses nothing.

  6. The plan card — read the routing line, then approve

    What I understood — the planner's own words plus the platform's point-by-point record of what was settled and how · what you'll get · numbered steps each with "done when" · what I will NOT do · assumptions first · the price (an unpriced side is SAID, never a silent $0). WHO DOES IT tells you the routing truth: on this walk it read "This plan writes files, and "release-notes-writer" cannot write it (a read-only workspace, or no editing tool granted)" — a specialist matched the work and was refused the equipment, so the generalist runs it. That sentence is the platform refusing to hand billed write-work to something that cannot write (the wrote-nothing era is over). The full plan is drafted in one piece and can take a few minutes; the page says so while it works, and if the drafting session dies the page says that too — it heals on its own, no button pressed. Assumptions carry their content: a point you skipped shows what was actually assumed for it, and the platform's per-slot "assumed a sensible default" rows collapse into one line naming the skipped points — never a wall. Approve. The PIN step-up appears only on higher-stakes plans — a trivial LOW-stakes note approves without one, and that is the tier mechanics working, not a hole. When it does appear, the page scrolls you to an armed panel ("Armed — your PIN confirms it") with its own Confirm and a "Never mind — stand down" that leaves nothing done.

  7. Ending one — watch it run to DONE

    On a project WITH captured commands the task moves Backlog → Executing → Verifying → Done — and on a note-sized task the execute leg is seconds, so be looking at the board when you approve. The board's folds now speak (coldwalk fix W2-B1): a column holding cards below its scroll edge shows a "N more below" pill (which is also the scroll button), the row shows "N more stages" when columns sit past the right edge, and the search box matches status words — typing cancelled finds a cancelled task wherever it sits. Verification materializes the revision with its history stripped and runs the project's own captured commands against it; the judge's verdict landed SHIP-with-notes here and the card settled into Done under its project's group, wearing its real cost. The PLAN expander on the Done card shows the plan's steps and the recorded stage progress as sub-items. Click the card — the structured overlay holds the whole story: the confirmed spec with acceptance criteria, the stage rail with every park and resume, the deliverable (in-review, one revision, content hash), and receipts that itemize ceremony vs execution per model and lane — local Qwen calls at USD 0, frontier calls on the subscription lane marked UNPRICED, never a silent $0.

  8. Ending two — THE DOOR: verify parks on a card instead of wedging

    A first software task on a fresh project (no captured commands — the honest v0 state of every store born from the New-project door) cannot be verified: there is nothing to check it with. The old platform wedged forever here. Now the work executes, then verification STOPS and parks the task on a verification-infrastructure card in your Inbox. Read it: the summary names the exact missing thing — "no build, test or lint commands are captured for project "The recipe box", so there is nothing to check this work with — capture them for the project (its Commands), then retry" — above three plain what-this-means lines, the quarantine record, and two live verbs: Retry — run verification again and Cancel the task. On the board the task sits in Needs attention wearing "waiting on a person". Nothing was marked verified, nothing was delivered, and nothing runs again until you answer.

    • Answer Cancel the task (this walk did) and the task settles into Backlog wearing CANCELLED — OPEN FOR WHY — there is no Cancelled column, by your own ruling.
    • Retry is the door for the day the project HAS commands — capture them first, or the same card returns and says so again.
    • Defect: a black screen, a wedge, or a verify that "approves" work nothing checked.

  9. Honest smallprint — three quarantine cards you may find

    On this world's first verified task the check runner itself failed to compose its sandbox (a first-run platform gap: the state dir's check-work/ scratch dir was never created — logged as a backend finding, fixed on this world by creating it). The platform did the honest thing: it did NOT fake a verdict — each failed check filed a CHECK-INTEGRITY quarantine card in the Inbox — plain words first (the check could not RUN; this is not a verdict on the work), the machine's own error line kept underneath as the record — findings numbered, suite quarantined. Their suite verbs (fix / waive) land with a later build, so they render greyed with a note saying exactly that; the cards stand as the record and nothing is lost by leaving them open.

If a planner session dies mid-journey (it has happened on the verification walks that wrote this page): nothing is asked of you. The journey page itself says "the working session broke — it heals itself", the recovery ladder forks and re-drives the session at its next sweep, and the healed plan arrives minutes later — machine-only, no button pressed. Seeing that once is the platform keeping its word, not a defect to report.

If anything on any page ever breaks while drawing itself, you land on a recover card — "this page hit a fault", with the reason and a Try again — never a black screen, and never a dead app. The sidebar keeps working; F5 is never needed. A black screen IS a defect to report.

The walk — eleven surfaces, in order

  1. Mission control

    0 running and 0 queued — by design. Everything seeded either waits on a person (9 parked runs with open asks — the "waiting on you" count and the inbox link) or is already done (1). Nothing seeded is dispatchable, so a zero here is the honest reading, not a broken feed.

    • Defect: a meter shows a confident number it cannot know. Absence must read as absence.
    • Defect: anything claiming to be running or executing right now.

  2. The live board

    Ten seeded tasks across five real columns — Backlog · Executing · Verifying · Needs attention · Done — grouped by project, horizontally scrolling, never wrapping. Every task sits in a declared column; nine carry a waiting on a person chip because their runs are parked on open asks, one is in Done. There is no "moonshot" column any more — that was a test artifact, and it left the demo world.

    • Executing/Verifying columns hold parked tasks whose stage is executing/verifying — the chip on the card, not the column, says nothing is actually running.

  3. Task detail — t-ship

    Open this task specifically. Of the ten seeded tasks, only t-ship ("Ship the release notes") and t-brief carry deliverables. Scroll to its Deliverables section — six rows, and that section is the door to step 9.

    • On any other task, "This task has produced no deliverables yet" is the correct answer.

  4. Fleet

    Per-person and per-model meters, budget remainders.

    • These render the real stores' answers here, not the fixture doubles — so figures may be absent where the committed fixture bodies show one. That difference is honest.

  5. The approval inbox

    Ranked cards with tier mechanics. The seed includes a conformance card (high tier, answerable) and an effect card that is deliberately not answerable by you — it is its owner's decision, and the card says so.

    • Defect: a card that looks answerable but has no working answer, or a not-answerable card that fails to say why.

  6. Settings

    Every setting, generated from the registry: clamp bounds, restart badges, audit history.

  7. Settings → "This device"

    The push-enrolment panel, on the same settings page.

  8. Chat

    Empty under the dev fallback. Sign in as alice (PIN above) and the seeded sessions appear. Checking both states is the point of this step.

  9. The review surface — seven deliverables

    This is the one the gate leans on. Each deliverable proves a different part of it. Start with d-site — it is the side-by-side diff.

    The page now leads with the result (RA-B1, 2026-08-17). Before any diff, every deliverable opens on three things, in this order:

  10. Workforce

    The seed composes three workers, so this has rows here. A worker in draft with no active version must say why nothing is equipped, not render an empty card.

    • On a genuinely fresh host this page is empty, and that is also correct — "no standing army": machinery is composed when earned.

  11. Phone width — re-walk 1, 5, 6

    Narrow the window to a phone width. Phone-complete is a requirement for mission control, the inbox and settings. Diff review and settings-bulk only need to be desktop-comfortable.

Known before you start — do not debug these.

  • "Try it out" errors on the code deliverable. It answers {"error":"internal","detail":"project: git worktree: chdir projects/release-notes.git: no such file or directory"}. The seed mints revisions but never creates the project repository a preview launches from. S13.8 requires every type to answer a defined state (no-preview, requires-container-tier), so a raw internal error leaking a filesystem path is a finding for question C below — already logged, not yours to chase.
  • Meters and the price table differ from the committed fixtures. They render the real stores' answers here; the fixture world's doubles cannot reach a throwaway binary. Honest difference.
  • The tour and first-use hints are app behaviour, not seed data. "Show me around" in the header starts the tour; hints re-appear on a full reload.
